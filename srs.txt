1. Project Overview
1.1 Project purpose

CampusCare is a centralized student support and administrative ticket management platform designed for colleges and educational institutions.

The platform enables students to raise requests related to fees, attendance, ID cards, documents, certificates, and other administrative matters. Support staff can manage, prioritize, assign, process, and resolve these requests, while administrators can monitor operational performance, staff workload, ticket ageing, and SLA compliance.

The system aims to replace scattered communication through emails, phone calls, and in-person visits with a structured, transparent, and traceable support process.

1.2 Problem statement

Educational institutions receive a large number of student requests across multiple departments. When these requests are handled manually, several problems can occur:

Requests may be lost or overlooked.

Students may not know the current status of their requests.

Staff may not have clear ownership of pending issues.

Urgent requests may not receive timely attention.

Managers may lack visibility into overdue tickets and staff workloads.

There may be no reliable history of actions taken to resolve a request.

CampusCare addresses these problems through centralized ticket management, role-based access, workflow automation, SLA tracking, and operational reporting.

1.3 Project objectives

Provide students with a simple interface to raise and track support requests.

Enable staff to manage tickets with clear ownership and status tracking.

Help managers monitor workloads, pending requests, and overdue tickets.

Maintain a reliable history of ticket actions and status changes.

Provide meaningful dashboards and reports for operational decision-making.

Ensure secure access, data consistency, and a responsive user experience.

1.4 Scope

The application will include:

Authentication and role-based access control.

Student support portal.

Staff ticket management workspace.

Admin and manager dashboard.

Ticket creation, assignment, prioritization, and resolution.

Comments, internal notes, and activity history.

SLA monitoring and escalation indicators.

Notifications and reporting.

Search, filtering, sorting, and pagination.

Demo data and test accounts.

The initial version will focus on a single institution with multiple departments and support categories. Multi-institution tenancy, real email/SMS delivery, and external college ERP integration are outside the initial scope.

2. User Roles and Permissions

The system must support three primary roles.

2.1 Student

Students can:

Log in securely.

Create support tickets.

Select a category, subject, description, and priority.

View their own tickets and current status.

Reply to staff requests for additional information.

View public comments and ticket activity.

Receive notifications about ticket updates.

Reopen eligible resolved tickets.

View their personal ticket history.

Students must not be able to view another student's tickets, staff-only notes, or administrative information.

2.2 Support Staff

Support staff can:

View tickets assigned to them.

View tickets in their department if authorized.

Accept or begin work on assigned tickets.

Update ticket status and priority within their permissions.

Add public replies and private internal notes.

Request additional information from students.

Resolve tickets with a resolution summary.

View ticket history and SLA deadlines.

View their own workload and pending tasks.

Staff must not be able to modify user roles, access restricted administrative settings, or change another department's tickets without authorization.

2.3 Admin / Manager

Administrators and managers can:

View all tickets across authorized departments.

Assign and reassign tickets to staff.

Change priority and manage ticket status.

Manage staff accounts and department membership.

View organization-wide dashboard metrics.

Monitor SLA breaches, ageing, and pending tickets.

Access reports and export ticket data.

Configure ticket categories and SLA policies.

Review ticket activity and audit history.

Administrative actions must be recorded in the audit trail.

3. Functional Requirements
3.1 Authentication and user management

FR-001 — User login

The system must provide a secure login screen with email or username and password.

FR-002 — Role-based redirection

After login, users must be redirected to the appropriate dashboard based on their role.

FR-003 — User registration

Students may register using the institution's approved registration process. For the prototype, administrators may also create demo accounts.

FR-004 — Session management

The system must maintain authenticated sessions securely and provide logout functionality.

FR-005 — User profile

Users must be able to view their profile information, including name, email, role, department, and student or employee identifier where applicable.

FR-006 — Access control

All restricted actions must be validated on the server. Hiding buttons in the UI alone is insufficient.

3.2 Ticket creation and management

FR-007 — Create ticket

Students must be able to submit a ticket with:

Category.

Subject.

Detailed description.

Priority.

Optional attachment, if supported.

The system must validate required fields before accepting the request.

FR-008 — Unique ticket number

Each ticket must receive a unique, human-readable ticket number, such as CC-2026-00125.

FR-009 — Ticket listing

Users must see tickets according to their permissions:

Students: their own tickets.

Staff: assigned tickets and authorized department tickets.

Admins: all tickets within their authorized scope.

FR-010 — Ticket details

The ticket details page must display the ticket number, requester, category, subject, description, priority, status, assigned staff member, creation date, last update, SLA deadlines, comments, and activity history.

FR-011 — Ticket editing

Authorized users may update editable ticket fields. The system must preserve the original creation date and record significant changes in the activity history.

FR-012 — Search and filtering

The ticket list must support:

Search by ticket number, subject, or requester.

Filter by status, category, priority, assignee, and SLA state.

Sort by creation date, update date, priority, and deadline.

Pagination for larger ticket lists.

3.3 Assignment and ownership

FR-013 — Manual assignment

Admins and managers must be able to assign or reassign tickets to eligible staff members.

FR-014 — Assignment validation

A ticket may only be assigned to an active staff account authorized for the relevant department or category.

FR-015 — Ownership visibility

Each ticket must clearly display its current assignee and assignment status.

FR-016 — Automatic assignment suggestion

The system should suggest an eligible staff member based on category specialization and current open-ticket workload.

This is a recommendation, not a mandatory automatic assignment. An authorized manager can override the suggestion.

FR-017 — Reassignment history

Every assignment or reassignment must be recorded with the previous assignee, new assignee, actor, and timestamp.

3.4 Ticket status and lifecycle

The system must support the following statuses:

Meaning

FR-018 — Status transitions

The system must validate status transitions according to role and current ticket state.

FR-019 — Waiting for student

When staff request additional information, the ticket must move to Waiting for Student and record the request.

FR-020 — Student response

When a student responds to a waiting ticket, the system must record the reply and allow the ticket to return to In Progress.

FR-021 — Resolution summary

Staff must provide a resolution summary before resolving a ticket.

FR-022 — Closure

A resolved ticket may be closed by an authorized user or automatically after a configurable closure period.

FR-023 — Reopening

A student may reopen an eligible resolved or recently closed ticket within a configurable reopening window. The system must record the reason and preserve the earlier resolution history.

FR-024 — Invalid transitions

Invalid transitions must be rejected with a clear error message and must not modify the ticket.

3.5 Comments and communication

FR-025 — Public comments

Students and authorized staff may add public comments visible to the ticket participants.

FR-026 — Internal notes

Authorized staff and admins may add internal notes that are visible only to authorized staff.

FR-027 — Comment history

Comments must be displayed chronologically with the author, timestamp, and visibility type.

FR-028 — Comment validation

Empty comments must be rejected. The system must distinguish between a public reply and a private internal note.

FR-029 — Communication notifications

The system should notify relevant users when a new public reply, assignment, or significant status change occurs.

3.6 SLA management

FR-030 — SLA policies

The system must support configurable response and resolution targets based on ticket priority.

FR-031 — SLA deadlines

When a ticket is created, the system must calculate its first-response and resolution deadlines using the applicable SLA policy.

FR-032 — First-response tracking

The system must record when the first qualifying public staff response is made.

FR-033 — SLA status

Each ticket must display one of the following SLA states:

On Track.

Approaching Deadline.

Overdue.

Completed.

FR-034 — Overdue detection

Tickets past their applicable deadlines must be identifiable in ticket lists and dashboards.

FR-035 — Priority changes

When a ticket's priority changes, the system must recalculate its resolution deadline according to the defined policy and record the change.

FR-036 — SLA pause rules

For the prototype, resolution-time calculations may pause while a ticket is Waiting for Student. The system must clearly indicate whether this rule is enabled and apply it consistently.

First-response deadlines must not be reset simply because the ticket is reassigned.

3.7 Escalation and pending actions

FR-037 — Approaching SLA deadline

Tickets approaching their SLA deadline must be highlighted to staff and managers.

FR-038 — Escalation indicator

Overdue tickets must display a clear escalation indicator in the ticket list and dashboard.

FR-039 — Pending-action ownership

Each ticket must indicate whether the next action is expected from the student, staff, or manager.

FR-040 — Escalation visibility

Managers must be able to filter tickets by overdue state, time since last update, and pending-action owner.

For the prototype, escalation may be implemented through in-app indicators rather than external notification integrations.

3.8 Dashboard and analytics

FR-041 — Admin dashboard

The admin dashboard must display:

Total tickets.

Open tickets.

In-progress tickets.

Waiting-for-student tickets.

Resolved and closed tickets.

Overdue tickets.

Average resolution time.

SLA compliance rate.

FR-042 — Ticket distribution

The dashboard must show ticket distribution by status, category, and priority.

FR-043 — Staff workload

The dashboard must show open tickets assigned to each staff member and highlight unassigned or overloaded work.

FR-044 — Ticket ageing

The system must categorize pending tickets by age, such as under 24 hours, 1–3 days, 3–7 days, and over 7 days.

FR-045 — Staff dashboard

Staff must see their assigned tickets, pending tasks, approaching deadlines, and overdue tickets.

FR-046 — Student dashboard

Students must see their total tickets, active tickets, resolved tickets, and recent activity.

FR-047 — Reports

The system must provide reports for ticket volume, category distribution, status distribution, SLA compliance, and resolution time.

FR-048 — Date filtering

Dashboard metrics and reports should support date-range filtering.

FR-049 — Export

Admins should be able to export filtered ticket data as CSV.

3.9 Notifications

FR-050 — In-app notifications

The system must support in-app notifications for:

Ticket assignment.

New public reply.

Status changes.

Ticket resolution.

Reopening.

Approaching or breached SLA deadlines.

FR-051 — Notification center

Users must be able to view notifications, mark them as read, and navigate to the associated ticket.

FR-052 — Notification permissions

Notifications must only reveal information the receiving user is authorized to access.

External email, SMS, and WhatsApp delivery are outside the required initial scope.

3.10 Administration

FR-053 — Category management

Admins must be able to manage ticket categories such as Fees, Attendance, ID Cards, Documents, Certificates, and General Support.

FR-054 — SLA policy management

Admins must be able to configure response and resolution targets for each priority.

FR-055 — Staff management

Admins must be able to view staff accounts, activate or deactivate accounts, and manage department membership.

FR-056 — Audit history

The system must preserve a traceable record of significant ticket and administrative actions.

4.1 Primary ticket lifecycle
4.2 Business rules

BR-001: Every ticket must belong to one student requester.

BR-002: Every ticket must have a unique ticket number.

BR-003: A ticket must have a valid category, subject, description, priority, status, and creation timestamp.

BR-004: Only authorized staff or admins may assign tickets.

BR-005: Students may access only their own tickets.

BR-006: Internal notes must never be exposed to students.

BR-007: A ticket cannot be resolved without a resolution summary.

BR-008: Closed tickets cannot be modified through normal workflow operations. Reopening must follow the defined reopening rules.

BR-009: Every significant status, assignment, priority, and resolution change must create an audit record.

BR-010: SLA deadlines must be calculated consistently and displayed in the user's local time zone.

BR-011: Dashboard metrics must be derived from persisted data rather than hardcoded sample figures.

BR-012: Failed operations must not leave partial or inconsistent database changes.

5.9 Data integrity requirements

Primary keys and foreign keys must be configured.

Ticket numbers must be unique.

Required fields must be validated.

Passwords must never be stored in plain text.

Ticket history must be append-only for ordinary users.

Ticket creation and its initial history record should be committed atomically.

Appropriate indexes should be created for ticket number, status, priority, assignee, requester, and creation date.

Secure authentication.

Role-based authorization enforced on the backend.

Reusable UI components.

Centralized API and error handling.

14. Assumptions and Trade-offs

The following assumptions guide the prototype:

The system serves one institution with multiple departments.

Users have one primary role in the initial version.

Students can access only their own tickets.

Staff can access tickets within their authorized scope.

Managers can access organization-wide ticket information.

SLA targets are configurable and initially use elapsed clock time.

In-app notifications are sufficient for the initial prototype.

File attachments are optional and may be omitted if they compromise core functionality.

Automatic assignment is a suggestion feature rather than a mandatory automated workflow.

The application is a functional assessment prototype, not a production-ready enterprise deployment.
